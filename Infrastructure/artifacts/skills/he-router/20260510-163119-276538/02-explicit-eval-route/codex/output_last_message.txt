Yes. The usual proof that a completed milestone is safe to close in Linear is a small evidence bundle showing:

- The scoped work is finished
- The acceptance criteria are met
- The relevant code/docs were validated
- No follow-up blockers remain

A solid closeout proof set looks like this:

1. `Task completion`
- Linked issues/subtasks are all done or intentionally moved out of scope.
- Any remaining items are explicitly documented as non-blocking follow-up.

2. `Validation evidence`
- Tests passed, with the exact command(s) and result.
- If applicable: build, lint, typecheck, or deployment checks passed.
- If relevant: screenshots, recordings, or QA notes for UI work.

3. `Change proof`
- The final diff only contains the intended milestone scope.
- Any risky areas were reviewed or called out explicitly.

4. `Operational safety`
- No known regressions, open incidents, or unresolved dependencies.
- Rollback path is understood if the work shipped.

5. `Closure note`
- A short summary in Linear stating what was delivered, how it was verified, and why closure is safe.
