2. **Classify the change as a lifecycle workflow**

   The workflow should be modeled around explicit account states, not payment events alone. I’d expect a production-grade account lifecycle to define something like:

   - `prospect` or `trialing`
   - `active`
   - `past_due`
   - `restricted`
   - `suspended`
   - `canceling`
   - `canceled`
   - `deleted` or `retained_for_compliance`

   The billing provider should update or propose state transitions, but the app should own account access decisions. That keeps payment infrastructure from becoming the permissions system.

3. **Separate three boundaries**

   **Account lifecycle boundary:** owns account state, billing status, plan entitlement, grace periods, cancellation behavior, and reactivation.

   **Permissions boundary:** owns who can view billing, change payment methods, upgrade/downgrade plans, cancel, invite users, or access paid features.

   **Persistence boundary:** owns durable records, audit history, idempotency keys, webhook event records, subscription snapshots, and any local entitlement cache.

   The production-grade version should make these boundaries explicit before implementation. Ambiguity here is where subtle billing bugs usually enter.

4. **Use Stripe guidance after the internal model is clear**

   Since this is billing-related, I’d bring in the `build-web-apps:stripe-best-practices` skill if the implementation touches Stripe objects, Checkout, Billing Portal, subscriptions, Connect, webhooks, invoices, payment intents, or customer records.

   I would not let Stripe object names define the domain model directly. The likely rule should be:

   - Stripe owns payment collection and subscription event source data.
   - The app owns account lifecycle and authorization.
   - Persistence stores enough provider data to reconcile, audit, and retry safely.

**Concrete Acceptance Criteria**

I’d consider the routing successful when the change answers these questions unambiguously:

- What is the source of truth for account state?
- Which roles can mutate billing state?
- Which billing events change app access?

- Which records are durable versus derived/cacheable?
- How are webhooks deduplicated and replayed?
- What audit trail exists for billing-sensitive changes?
- What tests prove unauthorized users cannot change lifecycle or billing state?
