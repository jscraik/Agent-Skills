   - In [#17540](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/17540), the issue explicitly says `logs_2.sqlite` shows repeated `thread/resume` activity around launch/resume windows.

2. It is storing huge failure payloads, not just compact status records.
   - In [#17880](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/17880), the issue says `logs_2.sqlite` contains the initial compact-task failure and then captures massive Cloudflare HTML challenge payloads in `feedback_log_body`.
   - The report calls out an exact error before truncation: `Error running remote compact task: You've hit your usage limit. > Re-try`
   - It also says the payloads are `30kb+` and that the app is consuming `~36,000+ character` CAPTCHA HTML blocks instead of JSON. That means each failed retry can write a very large blob into the DB.
   - Relevant evidence lines: `File: ~/.codex/logs_2.sqlite (table: logs)` and `Querying the feedback_log_body column ... reveals massive 30kb+ Cloudflare defensive payloads` in [#17880](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/17880#issuecomment-?).

3. Long-running Codex SQLite stores do not automatically shrink just because old rows stop being useful.
   - In [#16270](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/16270), Codex users reported that SQLite databases grew to extreme sizes on long-running installs.
   - The report explicitly says retention changes only affect new writes, and that `SQLite does not reclaim disk space after DELETE without an explicit VACUUM`.
   - That means once `logs_2.sqlite` grows, it can stay large even if older rows are later pruned.

So the evidence-based answer is:

- `logs_2.sqlite` gets huge because it is repeatedly written with session/thread resume events and retry loops, and in failure cases it can store very large HTML payloads.
- Even after old data becomes stale, SQLite will not automatically give the disk space back unless the app vacuums or rebuilds the DB.
