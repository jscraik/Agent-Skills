**Top Findings (Trust Gaps)**
1. `Critical`: A single `Approve` click hides consequences. Enterprise admins need to see scope, timing, and blast radius before commitment.
2. `High`: No explicit reversibility signal. If users don’t know they can undo/rollback, perceived risk spikes.
3. `High`: Missing accountability cues. Admins expect audit log, actor attribution, and policy references at decision time.
4. `Medium`: Button label is too vague. `Approve` doesn’t say what is being approved or what changes immediately.

**Recommended Interaction (Better Pattern)**
Replace one-step approval with a **two-step “Review and Approve” flow**:

1. Card CTA becomes **`Review & Approve`** (not `Approve`).
2. Clicking opens a side panel/modal with a **preflight summary**:
   - **What changes** (diff-style bullets)
   - **Who/what is affected** (counts + links)
   - **When it takes effect** (now/scheduled)
   - **Risk level** (low/medium/high)
   - **Rollback** (“Can be reverted within X” or “Irreversible”)
   - **Audit trail** (“This action will be logged as Jane Doe at 2:14 PM UTC”)
3. Confirmation action uses explicit copy:  
   - **`Approve 24 access requests`** or **`Approve policy update`**
4. Post-action state shows:
   - Success summary
   - **`Undo`** (if possible, time-bounded)
   - Link to audit log entry

**Microcopy Example**
- Before click: “Approving will grant `Billing Admin` to 24 users in `EMEA-Finance`.”
- Confirm button: “Approve 24 role grants”
- Support text: “Logged to Audit Trail • Reversible for 15 minutes”

**Why this works**
This interaction shifts trust from “button faith” to **predictability + control + accountability**, which is exactly what enterprise admins need before high-impact actions.
